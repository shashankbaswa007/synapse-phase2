## 1. Overview and Mapping Methodology

External validation requires establishing mathematical, temporal, and clinical compatibility between the synthetic records generated by SYNAPSE Phase 2 and real-world reference datasets. Conflating incompatible clinical quantities—such as comparing visit-level adherence with cumulative patient escalation, or comparing cross-sectional point prevalence with 2-year cumulative period risk—produces invalid validation claims.

This comparability matrix evaluates the mapping between **SYNAPSE Phase 2 Exported Tables** (`patients.csv`, `encounters.csv`, `conditions.csv`, `medications.csv`, `observations.csv`, `care_plans.csv`, `deaths.csv`) and the six candidate external datasets inventoried in [external_reference_dataset_inventory.md](file:///Users/shashi/synapse/synapse-phase2/docs/research_alignment/external_reference_dataset_inventory.md).

### Standardization of Comparability Classifications
Every comparison between a synthetic outcome and an external reference source is classified into one of five mutually exclusive categories:
- **`Directly Comparable`:** The synthetic estimand, population eligibility, time origin, and measurement protocol match the reference dataset without alteration.
- **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`:** Comparability is achievable after applying an explicit mathematical transformation (e.g. age standardization, age-truncation to $\ge 45\text{ years}$, unit conversion, or time-window alignment).
- **`Aggregate-Only Comparison`:** Only published group summary statistics (means, proportions, survival curves) are accessible; individual-level microdata rows are unavailable.
- **`Not Comparable`:** The synthetic and real-world quantities reflect fundamentally divergent clinical mechanisms or study designs (e.g. facility scheduled protocol vs. community health-seeking behavior; closed discrete-event steps vs. open-population churn).
- **`Data Unavailable`:** The clinical or biological variable is not collected in the candidate reference dataset.

## 3. Systematic Outcome-by-Outcome Comparability Evaluation

### 3.1 Age and Sex Distributions
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Proportion of male patients ($51.5\%$) and distribution across standard Census age bands ($0\text{--}14: 25.2\%$, $15\text{--}24: 17.8\%$, $25\text{--}44: 31.2\%$, $45\text{--}64: 18.8\%$, $65+: 7.0\%$).
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* LASI is restricted to adults aged $\ge 45\text{ years}$ and their spouses. To evaluate demographic fidelity, the synthetic cohort must be truncated to patients aged $\ge 45\text{ years}$ and re-weighted according to the national SRS older-adult pyramid.
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* CARRS includes adults aged $\ge 20\text{ years}$ in urban Delhi and Chennai. Truncating SYNAPSE to adults $\ge 20$ allows direct comparison against CARRS urban age strata.
- **Comparability with NFHS-5:**
  - *Classification:* **`Directly Comparable`**
  - *Details:* Individual weights in NFHS-5 enable direct national demographic pyramid comparisons for age bands $15\text{--}54$.

---

### 3.2 Baseline Hypertension Prevalence
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Proportion of eligible adults (age $\ge 20$) diagnosed with essential hypertension ($I10$) or SBP $\ge 140\text{ mmHg}$ at baseline ($t \le 31\text{d}$). Synthetic baseline point prevalence = **$32.53\%$** (95% CI: $27.7\%\text{--}37.8\%$).
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* In LASI (adults aged $\ge 45$), empirical hypertension prevalence is substantially higher ($\sim 45\%\text{--}52\%$). SYNAPSE adult cohort must be filtered to $age \ge 45$ to evaluate against LASI older-adult prevalence.
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Aggregate-Only Comparison`**
  - *Details:* CARRS reports baseline adult hypertension prevalence of $38.4\%$ in urban Delhi and $32.6\%$ in Chennai. SYNAPSE's $32.53\%$ baseline is directly aligned, but comparison is restricted to published aggregate figures unless microdata is approved.
- **Comparability with NFHS-5:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* NFHS-5 age structure is concentrated among individuals aged $15\text{--}49$. Applying direct age-standardization to the 2021 Census population yields an age-adjusted national prevalence of $\sim 28.5\%\text{--}31.0\%$.

---

### 3.3 Baseline Type 2 Diabetes Prevalence
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Proportion of eligible adults (age $\ge 20$) diagnosed with type 2 diabetes ($E11.9$) at baseline ($t \le 31\text{d}$). Synthetic baseline prevalence = **$11.8\%\text{--}12.9\%$**.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* In LASI adults aged $\ge 45$, diabetes prevalence is $\sim 19.5\%$ (higher due to age enrichment). Comparing synthetic patients aged $\ge 45$ against LASI self-reported plus DBS HbA1c-confirmed diabetes provides a valid transformed comparison.
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Aggregate-Only Comparison`**
  - *Details:* CARRS urban baseline diabetes prevalence is $\sim 16.2\%$ (Delhi) and $\sim 21.0\%$ (Chennai). Urban rates exceed national averages; synthetic national baseline ($11.4\%$) reflects rural-urban pooled census weights.
- **Comparability with NFHS-5:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* NFHS-5 measured random blood glucose (RBG). Patients with $\text{RBG} > 200\text{ mg/dL}$ or on medication must be calibrated against fasting/OGTT standards.

---

### 3.4 Disease Incidence Among Initially Disease-Free Participants
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Cumulative 2-year attack rate of new diagnoses occurring among patients free of the disease at $t_0$:
  - Hypertension: $\sim 3.64\%$ over 2 years ($\sim 1.8\%\text{ per year}$).
  - Type 2 Diabetes: $\sim 1.5\%\text{ per year}$ hazard in normoglycemic adults.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Data Unavailable`**
  - *Reasoning:* Wave 1 is cross-sectional with retrospective recall of diagnosis age, which is prone to severe recall telescoping. True prospective incidence requires Wave 2 release.
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Directly Comparable (Methodologically)` / `Aggregate-Only (Practically)`**
  - *Estimand:* CARRS measures prospective annual incidence among non-diabetic adults ($1.5\text{--}2.2\text{ per 100 person-years}$) and normotensive adults ($3.5\text{--}4.2\text{ per 100 person-years}$). The synthetic estimands match the CARRS prospective definitions exactly, but empirical verification is currently restricted to aggregate published rates.
- **Comparability with IHCI Registry:**
  - *Classification:* **`Not Comparable`**
  - *Reasoning:* IHCI only registers patients *after* they are diagnosed with hypertension; it does not follow a disease-free denominator.

---

### 3.5 Blood Pressure Distributions and Longitudinal Measurements
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Mean SBP at screening ($156.5\text{ mmHg}$), follow-up SBP ($138.2\text{ mmHg}$), and within-patient blood pressure change post-treatment.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Directly Comparable`**
  - *Details:* LASI contains three discrete, standardized Omron blood pressure measurements for every respondent. The continuous SBP and DBP distributions among untreated vs. treated individuals can be compared directly against synthetic observations using the 2-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and Wasserstein distance.
- **Comparability with IHCI Registry:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Details:* IHCI tracks clinical blood pressure readings at successive visits. Because IHCI captures real-world clinic noise and digit preference (rounding to $0$ or $5$), synthetic continuous distributions must be binned into standard $10\text{ mmHg}$ clinical intervals for valid comparison.

---

### 3.6 Glycated Hemoglobin ($\text{HbA1c}$) Distributions
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Simulated $\text{HbA1c}$ readings in diabetic patients during quarterly surveillance visits ($6.4\%\text{--}9.2\%$).
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Transformation:* LASI measured HbA1c from Dried Blood Spots (DBS). DBS HbA1c exhibits a slight laboratory shift relative to centralized venous blood testing; standard conversion equations published by LASI investigators must be applied.
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Directly Comparable (Methodologically)` / `Aggregate-Only`**
  - *Details:* CARRS uses centralized venous HPLC for HbA1c. Perfectly comparable in clinical definition, but microdata access is restricted.

---

### 3.7 Treatment Escalation and Intensification
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Cumulative proportion of treated chronic patients stepped up to dual therapy over 2 years (**$78.97\%$** across 5 seeds). Mean time to first intensification = **$123.4\text{ days}$**. Redundant duplicate orders = **$0$**.
- **Comparability with IHCI Clinical Registry:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation` (Estimand Divergence Disclosure)**
  - *Details:* In IHCI, protocolized step-up (adding Telmisartan to Amlodipine) is recorded at routine visits. However, real-world physician therapeutic inertia limits clinic-level intensification to $30\%\text{--}40\%$, whereas SYNAPSE executes strict guideline compliance without inertia ($79\%$). A valid comparison requires either introducing an empirical inertia parameter ($\gamma \approx 0.40$) into SYNAPSE or evaluating only conditional compliance given the decision to escalate.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Aggregate-Only / Cross-Sectional Regimen Distribution`**
  - *Details:* LASI records whether a patient is currently taking 1, 2, or $\ge 3$ antihypertensive drugs. The cross-sectional distribution of monotherapy vs. dual therapy can be compared against synthetic 2-year cross-sectional slices.

---

### 3.8 Follow-up Intervals (Inter-Encounter Durations)
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Mean interval between visits for multi-visit patients = **$123.4\text{ days}$** ($\text{SD} = 6.6\text{d}$).
- **Comparability with IHCI Registry:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Details:* IHCI patients receive 30-day or 60-day medication refills. Empirical inter-visit intervals average $45\text{--}75\text{ days}$. SYNAPSE's scheduled surveillance ($90\text{d}$ for T2D, $180\text{d}$ for HTN) represents idealized clinical protocols rather than actual public refill cadences.
- **Comparability with LASI / NFHS-5:**
  - *Classification:* **`Not Comparable`**
  - *Reasoning:* Population surveys do not record individual outpatient appointment timestamps.

---

### 3.9 Chronic Complication Incidence
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Cumulative 2-year incidence of secondary target-organ damage (Hypertensive CKD $I12.9$ and Diabetic Nephropathy $E11.2$) in chronic patients = **$2.88\%$** ($21/721\text{ patient-trials}$).
- **Comparability with CARRS Cohort:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Details:* CARRS defines incident nephropathy as new-onset microalbuminuria (uACR $\ge 30\text{ mg/g}$) or eGFR $< 60\text{ mL/min/1.73m}^2$ over longitudinal follow-up (incidence $\sim 3.0\%\text{--}5.5\%$ over 2–3 years). The synthetic estimand matches this empirical risk, but SYNAPSE currently generates complications via discrete Markov probabilities rather than continuous physiological biomarkers.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Aggregate-Only Comparison`**
  - *Details:* Wave 1 provides self-reported doctor-diagnosed chronic kidney disease prevalence among older diabetic/hypertensive adults ($\sim 4.2\%$).

---

### 3.10 Mortality and Survival
- **SYNAPSE Estimand:** Annual crude mortality rate ($0.0144\text{ yr}^{-1}$, $14.0\text{ deaths}$ per 500 patients over 720 days). $100\%$ cause code `R99`.
- **Comparability with Census SRS Life Tables:**
  - *Classification:* **`Directly Comparable`**
  - *Details:* Life-table stepping in SYNAPSE is mathematically derived from SRS 2016–2020 age-specific hazard rates $q_x(\text{age})$. Reconstructed expected deaths ($11.68$) and observed deaths ($14.00$) fall within the Poisson-Binomial 95% predictive interval.
- **Comparability with LASI Wave 1:**
  - *Classification:* **`Comparable After Documented Transformation`**
  - *Details:* LASI vital status tracking between Wave 1 and Wave 2 provides an older-adult ($45+$) 2-year mortality rate. Truncating synthetic data to $age \ge 45$ allows a direct empirical survival comparison.

### Actionable Conclusion
No single external reference dataset is capable of simultaneously validating all ten dimensions of SYNAPSE Phase 2:
- **LASI Wave 1** is the optimal dataset for **individual-level microdata evaluation** of older-adult baseline prevalences, continuous blood pressure distributions, laboratory $\text{HbA1c}$, and cross-sectional drug regimens.
- **CARRS** is the optimal clinical reference for **true longitudinal incidence and complication hazards**, but is currently limited to aggregate-level comparisons.
- **IHCI** is the optimal benchmark for **real-world clinical treatment escalation and visit intervals**, but demonstrates substantial clinical inertia that synthetic guideline rules do not currently model.
