## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

To evaluate the real-world fidelity of the synthetic longitudinal patient records generated by SYNAPSE Phase 2, the simulation must be benchmarked against external, independent healthcare datasets. 

This inventory audits six prominent candidate reference datasets across India and international benchmarks. For each candidate, we examine population representation, clinical variable depth, longitudinal follow-up granularity, data-access pathways, governance restrictions, and whether the dataset has already informed SYNAPSE Phase 2 model calibration.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A dataset is **not** accessible merely because a scientific paper describing it has been published. This inventory rigorously distinguishes publicly downloadable microdata from datasets requiring formal Institutional Review Board (IRB) protocols, Data Access Committee (DAC) approval, or restricted government data-use agreements.

## 3. In-Depth Candidate Dataset Profiles

### 3.1 Longitudinal Aging Study in India (LASI) — Wave 1 (2017–2018)

- **Source Organizations:** International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS), Mumbai; Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health; University of Southern California (USC).
- **Primary Publication:** International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS), *Longitudinal Ageing Study in India (LASI) Wave 1, 2017–18, India Report*, Mumbai: IIPS, 2020.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** Nationally representative survey covering all 28 States and 7 Union Territories of India (72,250 individuals across 42,949 households).
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** Multistage stratified cluster sampling of resident adults aged $\ge 45\text{ years}$ and their spouses (regardless of age).
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** Wave 1 fieldwork conducted 2017–2018. Designed as a prospective panel followed biennially (Wave 2 fieldwork completed, data processing ongoing). Includes retrospective self-reported year/age of diagnosis.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:**
  - *Demographics:* Exact age, sex, urban/rural residence, education, caste, marital status, household consumption.
  - *Vitals & Physical Measures:* Three seated blood pressure readings taken at 5-minute intervals using Omron HEM-7121 digital monitors; pulse rate; height; weight; waist and hip circumference.
  - *Biomarkers (Dried Blood Spots, $n \approx 25,000$):* Glycated hemoglobin ($\text{HbA1c}$), fasting blood glucose, high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), serum creatinine, total cholesterol, triglycerides, hemoglobin.
  - *Chronic Conditions:* Self-reported physician diagnosis of hypertension, diabetes, chronic heart disease, stroke, chronic kidney disease (CKD), asthma, COPD, cancer.
  - *Medications & Healthcare:* Specific rosters of prescribed daily medications for hypertension, oral hypoglycemic agents, insulin, statins, antiplatelet therapy; adherence challenges; public vs. private outpatient visits; inpatient admissions; out-of-pocket expenditure.
  - *Complications & Function:* Vision impairment, peripheral neuropathy symptoms, self-reported kidney disease, Activities of Daily Living (ADL), Instrumental ADL.
  - *Mortality:* Wave-to-wave vital status tracking and verbal autopsy protocols.
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* Fully accessible de-identified individual-level microdata available to researchers via the **Gateway to Global Aging Data** ([g2aging.org](https://g2aging.org)) and the IIPS data portal.
  - *Requirements:* Free online user registration, submission of a brief research abstract, and electronic agreement to the Data Use Agreement (prohibiting commercial use and re-identification).
  - *Turnaround Time:* Immediate to 24 hours.
- **Data Granularity:** Individual patient-level microdata (`.dta`, `.csv`, `.sas7bdat`).
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **Completely Independent.** LASI has never been ingested or used to tune any parameter in SYNAPSE Phase 2.
- **Evaluation Utility:** Ideal candidate for evaluating older-adult ($45+$) joint hypertension-diabetes comorbidity, multi-stage medication regimens, repeated blood pressure distributions, and laboratory-measured HbA1c distributions.

### 3.2 Center for Cardiometabolic Risk Reduction in South Asia (CARRS Cohort Study)

- **Source Organizations:** Public Health Foundation of India (PHFI), New Delhi; Centre for Chronic Disease Control (CCDC), New Delhi; Madras Diabetes Research Foundation (MDRF), Chennai; Emory University, Atlanta, GA, USA.
- **Primary Publications:** 
  - Nair et al., "Cohort Profile: The Center for Cardiometabolic Risk Reduction in South Asia (CARRS) Study," *International Journal of Epidemiology*, 2012; 41(6): 1615–1623.
  - Prabhakaran et al., "Incidence of Diabetes in South Asian Adults: The CARRS Cohort," *BMJ Open*, 2021; 11: e047468.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** Metropolitan Chennai and Delhi, India ($n = 12,271$ adults).
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** Multistage cluster random sampling of community-dwelling adults aged $\ge 20\text{ years}$ permanent residents of selected urban municipal wards.
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** Enrolled 2010–2011; followed **prospectively every year** with structured home visits, questionnaire tracking, and in-depth biochemical/clinical re-examinations every 2 years over a $6\text{--}10\text{ year}$ horizon.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:**
  - *Demographics:* Age, sex, socioeconomic status, dietary patterns, physical activity, tobacco/alcohol.
  - *Vitals & Physical Measures:* Standardized SBP and DBP (mean of 2nd and 3rd readings using Omron HEM-7080); anthropometry (BMI, waist-to-hip ratio).
  - *Laboratory Measures:* Annual fasting plasma glucose (FPG); 2-hour 75g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT); centralized HbA1c; lipid profile (total cholesterol, HDL, LDL, triglycerides); serum creatinine; urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR).
  - *Diagnostic Timestamps:* Longitudinal tracking of new-onset hypertension (SBP $\ge 140$ or DBP $\ge 90\text{ mmHg}$ or initiation of antihypertensives) and new-onset diabetes (FPG $\ge 126\text{ mg/dL}$ or $\text{HbA1c} \ge 6.5\%$ or initiation of hypoglycemic agents).
  - *Pharmacotherapy History:* Detailed medication rosters, dosage titration, monotherapy to dual therapy step-up, insulin initiation, and therapy switching.
  - *Target-Organ Complications:* Incident microalbuminuria, macroalbuminuria, CKD (eGFR $< 60\text{ mL/min/1.73m}^2$), retinopathy (fundus photography), peripheral neuropathy (monofilament testing), acute myocardial infarction, stroke.
  - *Mortality:* All-cause and cause-specific mortality confirmed through medical records, death certificates, and verbal autopsy.
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* **Restricted Academic Consortium Access.** Microdata is NOT publicly downloadable.
  - *Requirements:* Formal research proposal submitted to the CARRS Data Access and Publication Committee; co-investigator sponsorship from a participating institution; institutional IRB approval; bilateral Data Transfer Agreement (DTA).
  - *Turnaround Time:* $3\text{--}6\text{ months}$ minimum.
- **Data Granularity:** Individual-level longitudinal panel microdata exists internally, but external researchers typically only have access to published aggregate survival curves, incidence rates, and cross-tabulations.
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **Partially Leaked.** Published aggregate CARRS incidence rates were cited in [reference_data.py](file:///Users/shashi/synapse/synapse-phase2/synapse_simulator/evaluation/reference_data.py#L217-L238) (`inc_t2d_annual = 0.015`) to calibrate the diabetes incidence transition hazard. Consequently, CARRS aggregate incidence data cannot serve as an uncompromised external benchmark without risk of self-referential bias.

### 3.3 India Hypertension Control Initiative (IHCI) Operational Clinical Registry

- **Source Organizations:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India; Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR); World Health Organization (WHO) India Country Office; Resolve to Save Lives (RTSL).
- **Primary Publications:**
  - Kaur et al., "India Hypertension Control Initiative—Hypertension treatment and control in public healthcare facilities across 5 states," *Journal of Clinical Hypertension*, 2021; 23(4): 730–739.
  - ICMR & WHO India, *India Hypertension Control Initiative: Monitoring and Evaluation Report 2020–2022*, New Delhi: ICMR, 2022.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** Public sector Primary Health Centres (PHCs), Community Health Centres (CHCs), and District Hospitals across 100+ districts in Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Kerala, Telangana, Maharashtra, and other states.
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** Real-world routine clinical registry encompassing over **$2.1\text{ million}$** patients diagnosed with hypertension receiving routine outpatient care in government health facilities.
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** Continuous longitudinal registry (2018–present). Patients attend follow-up visits every 30 to 90 days for blood pressure monitoring and medication refills.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:**
  - *Clinical Workflow:* Exact visit timestamps, treating facility type (PHC vs. CHC vs. District Hospital).
  - *Blood Pressure:* SBP and DBP recorded at each routine monitoring encounter.
  - *Protocolized Medications:* Prescribed antihypertensive drugs according to standardized state treatment protocols:
    - Step 1: Amlodipine 5mg
    - Step 2: Amlodipine 10mg or Amlodipine 5mg + Telmisartan 40mg
    - Step 3: Amlodipine 5mg + Telmisartan 40mg + Chlorthalidone 12.5mg
  - *Treatment Trajectories:* Real-world clinical inertia metrics: proportion of patients with uncontrolled BP who receive medication intensification vs. those kept on unchanged monotherapy.
  - *Retention & Retention Loss:* 30-day, 90-day, and 180-day follow-up attendance, lost-to-follow-up (LTFU) classification, and clinic transfers.
  - *Complications & Mortality:* Acute CVD events and deaths recorded if reported to the health facility (substantial missingness on non-fatal community complications).
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* **Closed Government Health Registry.** Individual patient microdata (stored in the Simple / IHCI app backend) is strictly confidential government health records owned by the National Health Mission (NHM) and MoHFW.
  - *Requirements:* Access requires ministerial authorization, state NHM approvals, and formal ICMR collaborative agreements.
  - *Practical Availability:* Only quarterly aggregate district-level cohort monitoring reports and peer-reviewed published summary tables are practically available.
- **Data Granularity:** Aggregate quarterly cohort tables available publicly; microdata is administratively locked.
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **Documented Context.** Published IHCI clinical inertia statistics were referenced in [treatment_conformance_vs_fidelity.md](file:///Users/shashi/synapse/synapse-phase2/docs/research_alignment/treatment_conformance_vs_fidelity.md) to explain why synthetic intensification ($79\%$) exceeds real-world clinic intensification ($30\text{--}40\%$). It was not directly fitted or calibrated into simulation transition weights.

### 3.4 National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019–2021)

- **Source Organizations:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW), Government of India; International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS), Mumbai; ICF International (DHS Program).
- **Primary Publication:** International Institute for Population Sciences (IIPS) and ICF, *National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5), 2019–21: India*, Mumbai: IIPS, 2021.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** Comprehensive national coverage across all 28 States, 8 Union Territories, and 707 districts ($n = 825,954$ individual respondents).
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** Two-stage stratified random sampling of households; all women aged 15–49 and a subsample of men aged 15–54; biomarker module includes older adults in sampled households.
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** **Strictly Cross-Sectional.** Fieldwork conducted in two phases between June 2019 and April 2021. No longitudinal tracking of individual participants.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:**
  - *Demographics:* Age, sex, state, urban/rural, wealth index quintile, religion, caste.
  - *Blood Pressure:* Three standardized blood pressure readings taken with Omron digital monitors.
  - *Blood Glucose:* Random blood glucose (RBG) measured via finger-stick capillary blood using Accu-Chek Performa glucometers.
  - *Diagnosis & Medication:* Self-reported prior diagnosis of hypertension or diabetes; self-reported current medication use (binary yes/no).
  - *Complications:* None recorded.
  - *Mortality:* Household death roster in the past 12 months (aggregate recall; not individual longitudinal survival).
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* **Openly Accessible Public Microdata.** Available through the Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) Program ([dhsprogram.com](https://dhsprogram.com)).
  - *Requirements:* Registered DHS user account, project description submission, electronic acceptance of data-use agreement.
  - *Turnaround Time:* $24\text{--}48\text{ hours}$ for automated approval.
- **Data Granularity:** Individual-level microdata in STATA (`.dta`), SPSS (`.sav`), and flat text formats.
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **Completely Independent.** NFHS-5 was not used in Phase 2 calibration.
- **Evaluation Utility:** Highly valuable for independent multi-state cross-sectional point prevalence and blood pressure distribution evaluation; **incapable** of longitudinal trajectory, treatment intensification, or complication incidence validation.

### 3.5 ICMR-INDIAB National Study (2008–2020)

- **Source Organizations:** Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR); Madras Diabetes Research Foundation (MDRF).
- **Primary Publication:** Anjana et al., "Metabolic non-communicable disease health report of India: the ICMR-INDIAB national cross-sectional study," *The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology*, 2023; 11(7): 474–489.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** 31 States and Union Territories ($n = 113,043$ individuals).
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** Three-level stratified random sampling of community adults aged $\ge 20\text{ years}$.
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** Phased cross-sectional survey executed across four distinct geographical waves from 2008 to 2020.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:** Oral glucose tolerance tests (OGTT), HbA1c, fasting plasma glucose, standardized SBP/DBP, lipid parameters, self-reported diagnosis, treatment status.
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* **Proprietary Institutional Study.** Individual microdata is strictly held by ICMR and MDRF. No public repository or open application portal exists.
  - *Practical Availability:* Aggregate published tables, subgroup prevalence figures, and confidence intervals in *The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology* (2023).
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **HEAVILY LEAKED / PRIMARY CALIBRATION ANCHOR.**
  - Adult hypertension prevalence target ($30.7\%$, CI: $29.3\text{--}32.1\%$) was drawn from this paper.
  - Adult diabetes prevalence target ($11.4\%$, CI: $10.2\text{--}12.5\%$) was drawn from this paper.
  - Comorbidity co-occurrence ($50.1\%$ of diabetics have hypertension) was drawn from this paper.
- **Readiness Determination:** **DISQUALIFIED AS INDEPENDENT EXTERNAL VALIDATION DATA.** Testing SYNAPSE against ICMR-INDIAB would constitute circular calibration-target re-evaluation.

### 3.6 Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care (MIMIC-IV / MIMIC-IV-ED v2.2)

- **Source Organizations:** Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) Laboratory for Computational Physiology; Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC), Boston, MA, USA.
- **Primary Publication:** Johnson et al., "MIMIC-IV, a freely accessible electronic health record dataset," *Scientific Data*, 2023; 10: 1.
- **Geographic and Demographic Coverage:** Single-center tertiary academic medical center in Boston, Massachusetts, USA ($299,710$ unique patients; $431,231$ emergency and inpatient admissions).
- **Recruitment and Eligibility:** All patients admitted to BIDMC emergency department or intensive care units between 2008 and 2019.
- **Observation Period and Follow-up Frequency:** Longitudinal electronic health records covering consecutive hospitalizations, ED visits, and ICU stays over a multi-year window. Exact timestamps for orders, vitals, and lab draws.
- **Clinical and Biological Variables:**
  - High-resolution physiological monitoring (continuous SBP, DBP, heart rate).
  - Central laboratory tests (repeated HbA1c, serum creatinine, glucose, electrolytes).
  - Medication administration records (bar-code verified dispensing of antihypertensives, hypoglycemics, statins).
  - Diagnostic billing codes (ICD-9 and ICD-10 for essential hypertension, diabetic complications, acute kidney injury).
  - Long-term survival through linkage with state vital statistics.
- **Access Restrictions & Practical Availability:**
  - *Access Pathway:* **Credentialed Open Health Research Access via PhysioNet.**
  - *Requirements:* Completion of CITI training ("Data or Specimens Only Research"), identity verification, and signed Data Use Agreement strictly prohibiting re-identification.
  - *Turnaround Time:* $1\text{--}2\text{ weeks}$.
- **Prior Role in SYNAPSE Phase 2:** **Completely Independent.**
- **Evaluation Utility:** Provides an uncompromised software-architecture reference for longitudinal event sequencing, time-stamped medication step-ups, and laboratory-confirmed complications; however, because the clinical population is an **inpatient/acute cohort from the United States**, it cannot serve as a population-level epidemiological fidelity target for Indian outpatient chronic disease.

### Actionable Conclusion
Only **LASI Wave 1** provides immediate, uncompromised, individual-level empirical microdata for the Indian population that has zero calibration leakage with SYNAPSE Phase 2. CARRS represents the ideal longitudinal prospective cohort, but requires a formal multi-month data access committee application. ICMR-INDIAB is completely disqualified from external evaluation due to parameter leakage.
