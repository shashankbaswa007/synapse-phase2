## 1. Study Rationale & Prespecified Objective

This protocol defines the formal, prespecified methodology for independently evaluating the synthetic longitudinal electronic health records generated by SYNAPSE Phase 2 against external real-world clinical and epidemiological datasets.

### Fundamental Methodological Rules
1. **Prespecified Criteria:** All endpoints, statistical estimands, observation windows, censoring rules, and equivalence margins are established **in advance** of data ingestion.
2. **Strict No-Optimization Guarantee:** Model parameters, transition weights, and logic gates in SYNAPSE Phase 2 will **never** be fitted, calibrated, or tuned against the external validation datasets. Any modification in response to evaluation findings must be classified as a model revision and validated on a separate, fresh holdout.
3. **No Single Aggregate Score:** Overall "accuracy percentages" or composite single-number scores are scientifically invalid across multi-dimensional longitudinal systems. Each clinical outcome must be evaluated using statistical methods tailored to its specific data structure (e.g. survival analysis for time-to-event endpoints, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests for continuous vitals, and absolute risk differences for binary prevalences).

## 2. Target Populations & Eligibility Criteria

The evaluation protocol defines two distinct target cohorts to reflect available real-world data sources:

### Sub-Protocol A: General Adult Cohort ($\text{Age} \ge 20$) — CARRS / NFHS-5 Benchmark
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Resident individual in the simulated cohort.
  - Chronological age $\ge 20\text{ years}$ at observation start ($t_0$).
  - Valid entry date at or prior to $t_0$.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Pediatric individuals (age $< 20\text{ years}$).
  - Individuals deceased prior to $t_0$.
- **Synthetic Sample Size:** $N = 2,500$ adults (pooled across 5 random seeds of $N=500$ each).

### Sub-Protocol B: Older Adult Cohort ($\text{Age} \ge 45$) — LASI Wave 1 Benchmark
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  - Resident individual in the simulated cohort.
  - Chronological age $\ge 45\text{ years}$ at observation start ($t_0$).
  - Follow-up duration $\ge 720\text{ days}$ or terminated by death.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - Individuals aged $< 45\text{ years}$.
- **Synthetic Sample Size:** Truncated subset of adults aged $\ge 45$ (averaging $\sim 130\text{--}140$ patients per $500$-patient seed; $n \approx 700$ across 5 seeds).

## 3. Endpoints and Statistical Estimands

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

│                                                                                        │

│ 5. Longitudinal SBP and HbA1c Trajectory Slopes (Within-Patient Rate of Change)        │

│ 7. Two-Year All-Cause Actuarial Survival (Kaplan-Meier vs. Life Table)                 │

└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

### 3.1 Primary Endpoints

#### Endpoint 1: Continuous Blood Pressure Distributions (SBP and DBP)
- **Estimand:** Continuous cumulative probability distribution functions $F_{\text{syn}}(x)$ and $F_{\text{real}}(x)$ of systolic and diastolic blood pressure, stratified by:
  - Untreated screening readings (initial encounter).
  - Post-treatment follow-up readings (encounters at $t > 180\text{ days}$).
- **Statistical Method:**
  - Two-sample **Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test** for maximum vertical divergence:
    $$D_{\text{KS}} = \sup_x |F_{\text{syn}}(x) - F_{\text{real}}(x)|$$
  - **Wasserstein Distance ($W_1$)** for physical earth-mover deviation in $\text{mmHg}$:
    $$W_1(P, Q) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |F_{\text{syn}}(x) - F_{\text{real}}(x)| \, dx$$
- **Equivalence Margin:** $W_1 \le 4.0\text{ mmHg}$; $D_{\text{KS}} \le 0.08$.

#### Endpoint 2: Glycated Hemoglobin ($\text{HbA1c}$) Laboratory Distributions
- **Estimand:** Continuous density and deciles of $\text{HbA1c}$ readings among diagnosed diabetic patients.
- **Statistical Method:** Two-sample KS test and mean absolute difference across 10 decile bins.
- **Equivalence Margin:** Mean decile absolute difference $\le 0.40\% \text{ HbA1c}$; $D_{\text{KS}} \le 0.10$.

#### Endpoint 3: Age-Specific Baseline Point Prevalences
- **Estimand:** Binomial point prevalence $\hat{p}_k$ within 10-year age strata ($20\text{--}29, 30\text{--}39, 40\text{--}49, 50\text{--}59, 60\text{--}69, 70+$).
- **Statistical Method:** Stratum-specific absolute risk difference $\Delta_k = \hat{p}_{\text{syn}, k} - \hat{p}_{\text{real}, k}$ with Wilson 95% score confidence intervals.
- **Equivalence Margin:** $|\Delta_k| \le 5.0\text{ percentage points}$ for every stratum.

#### Endpoint 4: Cumulative Target-Organ Complication Incidence
- **Estimand:** 2-year cumulative incidence of secondary microvascular damage (CKD `I12.9` and diabetic nephropathy `E11.2`) among chronic patients disease-free at $t_0$.
- **Statistical Method:** Nonparametric Cumulative Incidence Function (CIF) accounting for the competing risk of all-cause mortality:
  $$I_k(t) = \int_0^t S(u-) \, d\Lambda_k(u)$$
- **Equivalence Margin:** Absolute incidence difference $\le 2.0\text{ percentage points}$ at Day 720.

---

### 3.2 Secondary Endpoints

#### Endpoint 5: Within-Patient Biomarker Trajectory Slopes
- **Estimand:** Annual rate of change in SBP ($\Delta\text{SBP}/\text{year}$) and HbA1c ($\Delta\text{HbA1c}/\text{year}$) among treated patients following medication initiation.
- **Statistical Method:** Linear mixed-effects regression with random patient intercepts and slopes:
  $$Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t_{ij} + b_{0i} + b_{1i} t_{ij} + \epsilon_{ij}$$
  Compare synthetic fixed-effect slope $\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{syn}}$ against empirical real-world slope $\hat{\beta}_1^{\text{real}}$.

#### Endpoint 6: Time-to-First Treatment Intensification
- **Estimand:** Time from initial monotherapy dispensation to stepped-up dual therapy prescription.
- **Statistical Method:** Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with right-censoring at death or study termination. Log-rank test and Cox Proportional Hazards ratio ($\text{HR}$).
- **Equivalence Margin:** $\text{HR} \in [0.75, 1.33]$ (disclosing expected divergence due to real-world clinical inertia).

#### Endpoint 7: All-Cause Survival
- **Estimand:** Cumulative 2-year survival probability $S(t) = P(T > t)$.
- **Statistical Method:** Kaplan-Meier curve comparison against Census SRS life-table survival.

## 4. Observation Window, Time Zero, and Censoring Rules

1. **Time Zero Alignment ($t_0$):**
   - For population cross-sectional metrics: $t_0$ is defined as `observation_start` (`2024-01-01 00:00:00`).
   - For longitudinal post-diagnosis trajectories: $t_0$ is anchored to the individual patient's **first recorded diagnostic encounter** (`encounters.start_time`).
   - For treatment intensification: $t_0$ is anchored to the **initial prescription date** of monotherapy.
2. **Observation Horizon:**
   - Standard window: Exactly $720.0\text{ days}$ ($1.97125\text{ Julian years}$).
   - External datasets with differing follow-up horizons (e.g. CARRS 3-year or 5-year visits) must be evaluated at exact common milestone intervals ($365\text{ days}$ and $720\text{ days}$).
3. **Censoring and Competing Risks:**
   - **Administrative Right-Censoring:** All patients alive at Day 720 are censored at $T = 720\text{ days}$.
   - **Competing Risk of Death:** Patients dying prior to Day 720 are recorded with their exact death timestamp. Mortality is treated as a competing event for all non-fatal clinical outcomes (complications, intensification, encounters) using cause-specific hazards and Fine-Gray sub-distribution hazards.

## 5. Missing Data and Real-World Measurement Noise

Real-world electronic health records and clinical cohorts suffer from substantial missingness, irregular visit schedules, and loss to follow-up ($10\text{--}25\%$). In contrast, discrete-event simulation outputs are complete.

The protocol specifies explicit rules for harmonizing measurement frequency:
1. **Digit Preference & Measurement Rounding:**
   - Clinical real-world blood pressure readings demonstrate strong digit preference (heaping on digits $0$ and $5$).
   - Synthetic continuous observations must be evaluated both in raw continuous form and after applying digit-rounding filters to match real-world measurement resolution.
2. **Visit Interval Matching:**
   - Real-world follow-up encounters do not occur on exact integer multiples of 90 or 180 days.
   - When evaluating visit intervals, synthetic observations must be matched against real-world clinical visit windows (e.g. $90 \pm 30\text{ days}$).
3. **Informative vs. Non-Informative Dropout:**
   - Real-world patients with poorly controlled disease may attend more frequent encounters or drop out entirely.
   - Sensitivity analyses must evaluate synthetic trajectories under simulated random loss-to-follow-up ($10\%$ and $20\%$ administrative dropout).

## 6. Simulation Replications & Monte Carlo Budget

To distinguish model stochasticity from systematic bias, all external evaluations must adhere to a formal Monte Carlo protocol:
- **Seed Replication:** A minimum of **10 independent random seeds** ($42, 101, 202, 303, 404, 505, 606, 707, 808, 909$).
- **Cohort Size:** $N = 1,000\text{ patients per seed}$ (total pooled synthetic population $N = 10,000$).
- **Reporting Requirement:** Every metric must report:
  - Point estimate (pooled mean across seeds).
  - Monte Carlo standard error ($\text{SE}_{\text{MC}} = s / \sqrt{M}$).
  - Empirical 95% simulation predictive interval ($2.5\text{th}$ to $97.5\text{th}$ percentiles).

## 7. Explicit Prespecified Failure Criteria

An external evaluation of SYNAPSE Phase 2 will be classified as a **Validation Failure (`FAIL`)** if any of the following occur:
1. **Invariant Failure:** Any synthetic patient record violates temporal causality (events pre-birth or post-mortem) or referential foreign-key integrity.
2. **Person-Time Violation:** Total simulated cohort person-time exceeds the theoretical bound $\sum \tau_i > N \cdot \tau_{\text{window}}$.
3. **Distributional Disparity:** SBP Wasserstein distance exceeds $W_1 > 8.0\text{ mmHg}$ or Kolmogorov-Smirnov test rejects distributional equivalence ($D_{\text{KS}} > 0.15$ with $p < 0.001$).
4. **Prevalence Divergence:** Stratified baseline point prevalence diverges from external reference data by $> 10.0\text{ percentage points}$ across more than two age strata.
5. **Mortality Inconsistency:** Observed deaths diverge from the individual Poisson-Binomial 99% predictive bounds.
