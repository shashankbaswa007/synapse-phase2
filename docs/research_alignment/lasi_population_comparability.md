## 1. Executive Summary & Rationale

Direct, unadjusted comparison of synthetic cohorts generated by SYNAPSE Phase 2 against real-world external datasets risks severe methodological bias. In particular, comparing an open-age national synthetic population against an older-adult cohort like the **Longitudinal Aging Study in India (LASI) Wave 1** would introduce massive confounding by age.

This document formally establishes:
1. Two distinct, non-overlapping target evaluation populations (Population A vs. Population B).
2. Explicit inclusion, exclusion, and censoring rules.
3. The mathematical methodology for direct age- and sex-standardization.
4. Systematic differences between community survey measurements and facility-based discrete-event clinical simulations.

## 2. Target Population Definitions & Eligibility

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        SYNAPSE EVALUATION POPULATIONS                                  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ POPULATION A: General Adults (Age >= 20)    │ POPULATION B: Older Adults (Age >= 45)    │
│ - Full adult demographic pyramid           │ - Aligned with LASI Wave 1 recruitment    │
│ - Benchmark: CARRS / NFHS-5                │ - Benchmark: LASI Wave 1 (IIPS/G2Aging)   │
│ - Status vs LASI: NOT COMPARABLE (< 45)    │ - Status vs LASI: DIRECTLY COMPARABLE     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 2.1 Population A: General Adults ($\text{Age} \ge 20$)
- **Purpose:** Evaluate demographic fidelity, baseline cardiometabolic prevalence, and primary care consultation patterns across the broader adult lifespan ($20\text{--}80+$ years).
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Resident individual in synthetic cohort or reference study.
  2. Chronological age $\ge 20.0\text{ years}$ at observation start ($t_0 = \text{2024-01-01}$).
  3. Valid recorded birth date and sex.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Pediatric individuals ($\text{age} < 20.0\text{ years}$).
  2. Individuals deceased prior to $t_0$.
- **LASI Wave 1 Comparability Verdict:**
  > [!WARNING]
  > **METHODOLOGICAL NON-COMPARABILITY OF POPULATION A**  
  > LASI Wave 1 was specifically designed as an aging study and restricted sampling to resident adults aged $\ge 45\text{ years}$ and their spouses. Resident spouses aged $< 45$ comprise $< 2\%$ of the total sample and are heavily selected (non-random marital clustering).  
  > **Evaluating Population A ($20\text{--}44\text{ years}$) against LASI is scientifically invalid and prohibited.** Extrapolating older-adult LASI sampling weights or disease prevalences to adults younger than 45 represents severe extrapolation bias.

### 2.2 Population B: Older Adults ($\text{Age} \ge 45$)
- **Purpose:** Rigorously evaluate older-adult cardiometabolic disease burden, multi-stage medication escalation, continuous blood pressure distributions, and laboratory-measured HbA1c against LASI Wave 1.
- **Inclusion Criteria:**
  1. Resident individual in synthetic cohort or LASI reference cohort.
  2. Chronological age $\ge 45.0\text{ years}$ at observation start ($t_0$).
  3. Minimum follow-up duration of $720\text{ days}$ or terminated by death.
  4. For biomarker endpoints: valid seated blood pressure or laboratory HbA1c.
- **Exclusion Criteria:**
  1. Individuals aged $< 45.0\text{ years}$.
- **LASI Wave 1 Comparability Verdict:**
  > [!NOTE]
  > **POPULATION B IS DIRECTLY COMPARABLE**  
  > Once the synthetic cohort is filtered to $\text{Age} \ge 45$, the synthetic denominator aligns exactly with the LASI sampling frame.

## 3. Mathematical Framework for Direct Standardization

Even within the $\text{Age} \ge 45$ population, differences in age structure (e.g. higher survival in synthetic simulations vs. real-world mortality attrition) can confound biological comparisons.

To eliminate structural confounding, all primary comparative metrics must report **both original unstandardized estimates and directly standardized estimates**.

### 3.1 Standard Population Reference Weights
We adopt the **Census of India Sample Registration System (SRS) 2021** older-adult population distribution as the external gold standard:

| Age Stratum ($k$) | Sex ($s$) | SRS Reference Population Weight ($w_{ks}$) | Normalized Weight Proportion |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **45–54** | Male | $34,812,000$ | $0.264$ ($26.4\%$) |
| **45–54** | Female | $33,758,000$ | $0.256$ ($25.6\%$) |
| **55–64** | Male | $24,792,000$ | $0.188$ ($18.8\%$) |
| **55–64** | Female | $24,001,000$ | $0.182$ ($18.2\%$) |
| **65–74** | Male | $7,650,000$ | $0.058$ ($5.8\%$) |
| **65–74** | Female | $6,860,000$ | $0.052$ ($5.2\%$) |
| **Total** | All $\ge 45$ | $131,873,000$ | $\mathbf{1.000}$ ($\mathbf{100.0\%}$) |

### 3.2 Direct Standardization Equation
For any continuous biological variable (e.g. SBP) or binary prevalence indicator (e.g. Hypertension), the directly standardized estimate $\hat{\theta}_{\text{std}}$ is defined as:

$$\hat{\theta}_{\text{std}} = \frac{\sum_{k} \sum_{s} w_{ks} \cdot \bar{Y}_{ks}}{\sum_{k} \sum_{s} w_{ks}}$$

Where:
- $w_{ks}$ is the SRS standard population weight for age group $k$ and sex $s$.
- $\bar{Y}_{ks}$ is the empirical stratum-specific mean or proportion observed in the cohort:
  $$\bar{Y}_{ks} = \frac{1}{n_{ks}} \sum_{i=1}^{n_{ks}} Y_{ksi}$$
- If a specific stratum in a synthetic replication contains 0 observations, weights are proportionally re-normalized across populated strata with explicit warning disclosure.

## 4. Measurement Differences: Community Survey vs. Clinical Simulation

A critical source of discrepancy in health data validation arises from conflating **epidemiological survey measurements** with **facility-based clinical observations**.

| Feature / Dimension | Real-World LASI Wave 1 Survey | SYNAPSE Phase 2 Clinical Simulation | Harmonization & Handling Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Measurement Setting** | Household community interview by non-physician field enumerators. | Formal outpatient clinic consultation room with medical officer. | Disclose setting divergence. Household readings may exhibit lower "white-coat" effect. |
| **Blood Pressure Protocol** | Three seated readings at 5-minute rest intervals using Omron HEM-7121. | Single seated clinical examination during doctor visit. | Synthetic blood pressure compared against protocolized average of 2nd and 3rd LASI readings. |
| **Digit Preference / Heaping**| Minimal digit preference due to automated digital oscillometric display. | Continuous floating-point values generated by differential equation. | Continuous distributions evaluated via Kolmogorov-Smirnov and 1-Wasserstein metrics. |
| **HbA1c Assay Method** | Dried Blood Spot (DBS) capillary blood on Whatman filter paper. | Venous blood sample processed via centralized laboratory HPLC. | Apply empirical calibration conversion: $\text{HbA1c}_{\text{venous}} = 0.94 \times \text{DBS} + 0.45$. |
| **Diagnostic Definition** | Self-reported recall of prior doctor diagnosis ("Has a doctor ever told you..."). | Explicit clinical diagnosis code (`I10`, `E11.9`) assigned at encounter. | Reference case definition combines self-report with objective biomarker thresholds. |
| **Follow-up Cadence** | Single cross-sectional survey contact in Wave 1. | Continuous discrete-event simulation tracking visits over 720 days. | Do NOT evaluate longitudinal rates (incidence, escalation) against cross-sectional Wave 1 data. |

## 5. Explicit Equivalence Margins and Threshold Justification

Statistical metrics require defensible equivalence margins:

1. **Continuous Blood Pressure Distribution:**
   - **Wasserstein Distance ($W_1$):** Provisional threshold $W_1 \le 4.0\text{ mmHg}$.
     - *Justification:* A $4\text{ mmHg}$ shift in population mean SBP corresponds to a clinically meaningful difference in cardiovascular event risk (Framingham / Oxford meta-analyses). Divergence $\le 4.0\text{ mmHg}$ indicates high distribution fidelity.
   - **Kolmogorov-Smirnov Statistic ($D_{\text{KS}}$):** Provisional threshold $D_{\text{KS}} \le 0.08$.
     - *Justification:* Maximum vertical cumulative probability divergence $\le 8\%$ ensures tails (hypertensive crises, hypotension) match empirical distributions.
2. **Laboratory HbA1c Distribution:**
   - **Wasserstein Distance:** $W_1 \le 0.60\% \text{ HbA1c}$.
   - **Total Variation Distance (TVD) on Categories (<7%, 7–8%, >8%):** $\text{TVD} \le 0.15$.
3. **Older-Adult Disease Prevalence:**
   - **Absolute Risk Difference:** $|\Delta| \le 6.0\text{ percentage points}$ for Hypertension; $|\Delta| \le 5.0\text{ percentage points}$ for Diabetes.
