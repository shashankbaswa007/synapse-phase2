# SYNAPSE Phase 2 — Real-World Reference Data Catalog

## 1. Scope & Evaluation Population Definition

This catalog establishes the empirical real-world ground truth benchmarks for validating synthetic healthcare datasets generated by SYNAPSE Phase 2.

### Target Population Characteristics
- **Geographic Scope:** Republic of India (`IN`), National Representative Baseline.
- **Eligible Age Range:** 0 to 100+ years.
- **Reference Observation Period:** 2020–2023 empirical publications and census reports.
- **Synthetic Simulation Window:** 2024-01-01 to 2026-01-01 (2.0 calendar years, 7-day timestep).
- **Supported Clinical Conditions:**
  - Essential (primary) hypertension (`I10`)
  - Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications (`E11.9`)
  - Acute upper respiratory infection, unspecified (`J06.9`)
  - Acute myocardial infarction death outcome (`I21.9`)

## 3. Denominator Alignment Rules

A major source of flawed statistical evaluations in synthetic healthcare data is **denominator mismatching**. The evaluation framework strictly enforces:

### 3.1 Non-Communicable Disease Adult Restrictions
- **Condition:** Essential Hypertension (`I10`) and Type 2 Diabetes (`E11.9`).
- **Real-World Methodology:** In the ICMR-INDIAB study (Lancet 2023), participants were required to be **$\ge 20$ years of age**.
- **Rule:** The synthetic denominator is strictly filtered to patients where `age >= 20`.
- **Warning on Crude Rates:** Computing crude whole-population prevalence ($K / N_{total}$) erroneously dilutes the rate by including pediatric patients (0–19 years, who represent ~35% of India's population). The evaluation framework reports crude rates purely as secondary context while keeping the age-standardized denominator authoritative for pass/fail determination.

### 3.2 Episodic Acute Condition Cohort Sizing
- **Condition:** Acute Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (`J06.9`).
- **Rule:** Evaluated across the entire cohort ($0 \le \text{age} \le 100$).
- **Methodology Distinction:** Acute URTI prevalence in surveys is typically assessed over a 2-week to 1-month recall window. In a 2-year longitudinal simulation, cumulative period prevalence measures the fraction of patients who experienced at least one episode during the 24-month observation window.

### 3.3 Comorbidity Denominator Conditioning
- **Metric:** Conditional prevalence $P(\text{Hypertension} \mid \text{Type 2 Diabetes})$.
- **Denominator:** Strictly the subset of adults aged $\ge 20$ who have an active diagnosis of `E11.9`.
- **Handling Empty Strata:** If no synthetic patient develops T2D (possible in very small cohorts $N < 10$), the metric status is set to `NOT_APPLICABLE` rather than raising a division-by-zero error or falsely reporting failure.

## 4. Known Differences Between Synthetic & Real-World Populations

To prevent unrealistic expectations of exact numerical equivalence, the following known structural divergences are documented:

1. **Facility vs. Community Cohorts:** Real-world epidemiological studies (such as ICMR-INDIAB) sample from the general community, capturing both diagnosed and undiagnosed individuals. The synthetic simulation models clinical interactions within an outpatient healthcare network where diagnoses are recorded upon presentation.
2. **Fixed 2-Year Surveillance:** The simulation models a 24-month discrete window. Episodic conditions accumulate higher cumulative incidence over 24 months than single-point cross-sectional surveys report.
3. **Adherence Assumption:** Simulated patients are assumed to follow prescribed regimens with 100% adherence unless explicitly modeled otherwise. Real-world Indian adherence for chronic anti-hypertensive therapy is documented at approximately 45%–55%.
4. **Finite Sample Stochastic Variance:** For cohorts of size $N=20$ or $N=100$, stochastic binomial sampling variance can produce wide confidence intervals (e.g. Wilson 95% CI for HTN at $N=20$ spans 26% to 81%).
