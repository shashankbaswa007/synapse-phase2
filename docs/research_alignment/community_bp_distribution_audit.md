## 1. Executive Summary

This audit evaluates the statistical, physiological, and epidemiological properties of blood pressure generated by the **Independent Community Health Assessment Pathway** (`community_assessments.csv`).

### Key Findings:
1. **Zero Physiological Invariant Violations:** All 561 community assessments satisfy strict physiological boundary constraints:
   - Systolic $\le$ Diastolic blood pressure: **0 violations (0.0%)**.
   - Pulse Pressure $< 20$ mmHg: **0 violations (0.0%)**.
   - Pulse Pressure $> 110$ mmHg: **0 violations (0.0%)**.
2. **Realistic Population-Level Moments:** The overall community cohort exhibits:
   - Mean Systolic Blood Pressure (SBP): **127.60 mmHg** (SD 9.83; Median 126.90; IQR 12.80).
   - Mean Diastolic Blood Pressure (DBP): **79.81 mmHg** (SD 5.42; Median 79.50; IQR 7.50).
   - Mean Pulse Pressure (PP): **47.78 mmHg** (SD 9.77; Median 47.20; IQR 15.00).
3. **Plausible Treatment Control Rates:** Among treated hypertensive individuals ($N = 233$), 58.80% achieved blood pressure control ($< 140/90$ mmHg) and 41.20% remained uncontrolled ($\ge 140$ or $\ge 90$ mmHg), closely matching the 60/40 target in `hypertension.yaml`.
4. **Primary Epidemiological Limitation — Absence of Age-Related Systolic Drift:** SBP remains virtually flat across age groups (~127.8 mmHg at 45–54 vs ~126.9 mmHg at 75+) because community values are sampled from stationary uniform intervals based solely on disease and treatment status. The model lacks continuous biological arterial stiffening.

## 5. Critical Physiological Appraisal: Isolated Systolic HTN & Age Effects

### 5.1 Phenomenological Occurrence vs. Biological Mechanism

In the community assessment cohort:
- Isolated Systolic Hypertension (ISH) occurs in **11.94%** of respondents (67 / 561).
- However, this occurs purely as an artifact of the upper bound of the treated uniform distribution ($\text{SBP} \in [120, 150]$) overlapping with the threshold $140$, while $\text{DBP} \in [75, 92]$ frequently falls below $90$.
- In genuine aging biology:
  1. Large elastic arteries (aorta and carotid) undergo elastin fragmentation, collagen cross-linking, and wall calcification.
  2. Aortic compliance declines, increasing pulse wave velocity (PWV) and causing early reflection of pulse waves.
  3. Consequently, central aortic systolic pressure rises while diastolic pressure peaks around age 55 and subsequently declines, causing progressive pulse pressure widening.
- In the current synthetic generator, pulse pressure remains flat across age groups:
  - 45–54 years: Mean PP = **48.15 mmHg**
  - 75+ years: Mean PP = **47.07 mmHg**

### 5.2 Scientific Governance Commitment

**DO NOT introduce arbitrary linear age coefficients or heuristic ratio hacks.**  
Introducing unvalidated ad-hoc equations (e.g. `sbp += 0.5 * age`) would violate the non-negotiable governance constraints of SYNAPSE Phase 2:
> *"Do not tune parameters to match the structural fixture. Do not alter clinical transition probabilities without documented evidence and explicit justification."*

### 5.3 Calibration Roadmap for Future Phases

When genuine authorized LASI Wave 1 microdata are ingested:
1. Extract empirical age- and sex-stratified joint quantiles of SBP and DBP from the reference dataset.
2. Formulate a continuous biological vascular impedance function grounded in peer-reviewed cardiovascular literature:
   $$\text{SBP}(a) = \text{SBP}_{\text{base}} + \beta_{\text{compliance}} \cdot \max(0, a - 45)^{\gamma}$$
3. Estimate $\beta_{\text{compliance}}$ and $\gamma$ strictly through minimum-distance estimation against the reference distribution.
4. Validate the resulting model using out-of-sample cross-validation and preserve the `REFERENCE_DATA_PENDING` gate until all prespecified statistical criteria are satisfied.
